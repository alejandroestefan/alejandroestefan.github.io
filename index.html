Research

My research is organized around two connected pillars: the capacity of the state to implement policy and reach its population, and the capacity of firms to adopt technologies and improve productivity.

Research pillar one · The reach and limits of government

State Capacity

How states enforce rules, mobilize revenue, reach populations beyond formal institutions, and turn ambitious policies into durable economic and social outcomes.

01
MexicoResubmitted to American Economic Review

Outsourcing Policy and Worker Outcomes: Causal Evidence from a Mexican Ban

with Roberto Gerhard, Joseph Kaboski, Illenin Kondo, and Wei Qian

Banning the outsourcing of core workers raised compensation and the labor share without increasing firms’ total labor costs. Effective labor regulation can therefore recover worker income and fiscal obligations without sacrificing productivity.

★ Citibank-Banamex Prize for Excellence in Economic Research, 2nd place, 2023

Working PaperVoxDev
02
MexicoRevised & Resubmitted to American Economic Journal: Applied Economics

Taxing Property in Developing Countries: Theory and Evidence from Mexico City

with Anne Brockmeyer, Karina Ramírez, and Juan Carlos Suárez-Serrato

Tax increases and enforcement letters raise revenue, but household liquidity constraints limit compliance. Building fiscal capacity requires both credible enforcement and tax design adapted to taxpayers’ ability to pay.

★ Citibank-Banamex Prize for Excellence in Economic Research, 2nd place, 2020 · ADB/IEA Innovative Policy Research Award, 1st place, 2023

Working PaperVoxDev
03
MexicoJournal of Development Studies, 2025

Women’s Labor Force Participation, Family Planning Policies, and Fertility: Causal Evidence from Mexico

Formal employment connected women to public healthcare and substantially expanded their use of government contraception. The state’s capacity to deliver social policy depends fundamentally on whom its institutions can reach.

Working PaperDOI

MexicoWorld Development Perspectives, 2026

U.S. Manufacturing Reallocation: Impacts on Mexican Labor Markets

Export manufacturing expanded low-skill assembly employment but generated neither wage growth nor spillovers to other tradable sectors. Industrial policy must promote upgrading and linkages, not employment expansion alone, to produce broader development.

Working PaperDOI
05
MexicoWork in Progress

Undoing Colonial Stratification: Higher Education and Indigenous Access to the Professions

Examines whether access to higher education can overcome persistent barriers that exclude Indigenous people from professional occupations and intergenerational mobility.

06
MexicoWork in Progress

The Impacts of Geographically Targeted Anti-Poverty Programs on Local Poverty and Voting Behavior: Evidence from Mexico

with Stephen Pitts

Two decades of geographically targeted conditional transfers did not produce lasting poverty reduction. State capacity depends not only on reaching beneficiaries, but on designing transfers that households value and can convert into durable gains.

Research pillar two · Firm growth, upgrading, and productivity

Private Sector Development

How firms enter, survive, grow, and upgrade, and how training, technology, finance, internal organization, and a safer operating environment can relax the constraints they face.

01
GuatemalaWorld Bank Economic Review, 2023

Online Training for Micro-Entrepreneurs: Experimental Evidence from Guatemala

with Martina Improta, Romina Ordoñez, and Paul Winters

Online training improved business capabilities when combined with individualized consulting. Scalable knowledge delivery requires complementary support that helps entrepreneurs translate learning into management practices and firm performance.

Working PaperDOI
02
GuatemalaJournal of Public Economics, 2025

The Effects of Extortion on Entrepreneurial Entry and Exit: Theory and Evidence from Guatemala

with Romina Ordoñez

Extortion discourages entrepreneurial entry and hastens firm exit, while affordable security technology substantially improves survival. A safer operating environment is a foundational input into entrepreneurship and firm growth, especially for women.

Working PaperDOI
03
EcuadorFood Policy, 2025

Hybrid Agricultural Extension and the Adoption of Climate-Resilient Varieties: Evidence from Oil Palm in the Amazon

with Romina Ordoñez and Paul Winters

Personalized digital nudges and in-person field days substituted for one another in transmitting knowledge, but only field days induced farmers to adopt climate-resilient crops and the practices needed to realize their benefits. Digital extension can spread information cheaply, while demonstration and social learning remain essential for action.

Working PaperDOI
04
ChileWorking Paper

Rank-Based Targeting and Development Impact: Evidence from Sustainable Agriculture in Chile

with Daniela Castillo, Jose Abel Echeverria, Nicole Guerrero, and Romina Ordoñez

Technical assistance combined with capital transfers increased sustainable practice adoption, with the largest gains among mid-ranked applicants. When beneficiaries need sufficient capacity to succeed but support is redundant for the most capable, rank-based targeting can outperform need-based allocation.

Working Paper
05
Cross-countrySubmitted

From Meta-Analysis to Cost-Effectiveness at Scale: An Application to Digital Business Training

with Álvaro Montoya and Paul Winters

A structural synthesis of sparse experimental evidence traces the weak pass-through of digital training to limited practice adoption. Combining digital content with targeted human support at that margin can generate larger profit gains per dollar than digital-only delivery.

Working Paper

Training Modality for Women Entrepreneurs and Development Impact: Experimental Evidence from Bolivia

with María Alejandra Barrientos Chávez and Patricia Yánez-Pagans

Tests whether virtual delivery or in-person training with childcare can remove participation barriers and translate business knowledge into productive practices for women entrepreneurs.

07
GuatemalaWork in Progress

Women-Led SMEs: Drivers of Failure and Success

with Paola Mallia, Paul Winters, and Yunrui Zhang

Examines which constraints and firm capabilities determine whether women-led enterprises survive, absorb new knowledge, and grow.

08
PeruWork in Progress

Internal Organization and Productivity Within the Farm: Theory and Experimental Evidence from Peru

with Gabriela Aparicio-Linale and María Alejandra Barrientos-Chávez

Tests whether training lead farmers together with key support workers improves coordination and productivity. Productive upgrading may depend on how knowledge is organized within the firm, not only on who receives it.
